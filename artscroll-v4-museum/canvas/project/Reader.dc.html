פרק ראשון — הכל מעריכין

Chapter One, Hakol Ma’arichin

מתני׳ הכל מעריכין ונערכין נודרין ונידרין כהנים לוים וישראלים נשים ועבדים

Mishnah. All may pledge a valuation and may be the subject of a valuation;1 all may vow another’s worth and may be the subject of such a vow: Kohanim, Leviim and Yisraelim, women and slaves. A valuation is the fixed sum the Torah sets for a person by age and sex, pledged to the Temple treasury. A vow of worth pledges what the person would fetch if sold. Every one of these may make either vow, and may be its subject.

1The valuations are set out in Leviticus 27:1–8, from five shekels for a boy under five to fifty for a man between twenty and sixty.

טומטום ואנדרוגינוס נודרין ונידרין ומעריכין אבל לא נערכין שאינו נערך אלא הזכר ודאי ונקבה ודאית

A tumtum and an androgynos2 may vow worth, may be the subject of a vow of worth, and may pledge a valuation, but they are not the subject of a valuation, for only one who is definitely male or definitely female is valued. The Torah gives one scale for a man and another for a woman, so a person who is neither definitely one nor the other has no sum to be valued at. A vow of worth depends only on market price, so it still applies.

2A tumtum is a person whose sex cannot be determined. An androgynos has the features of both sexes.

חרש שוטה וקטן נידרין ונערכין אבל לא נודרין ולא מעריכין מפני שאין בהם דעת:

A deaf-mute, a person who is not mentally competent, and a minor may be the subject of a vow of worth or a valuation, but they may not vow worth or pledge a valuation, because they lack legal understanding. Someone else may pledge their value, since a competent adult is making the vow. A vow of their own does not bind.

גמ׳ הכל מעריכין לאתויי מאי לאתויי מופלא סמוך לאיש

Gemara. “All may pledge a valuation.” What does “all” come to include? It includes a boy close to adulthood whose understanding has been confirmed.3 In the year before he comes of age, a boy who shows he understands what a vow means may pledge a valuation, though he is not yet an adult.

3Mufla samuch l’ish: a boy in his thirteenth year whose vows take effect once it is clear he knew to Whom he vowed.

נערכין לאתויי מאי לאתויי מנוול ומוכה שחין

“And may be the subject of a valuation.” What does this come to include? It includes a person who is disfigured or covered in boils. Such a person would fetch little on the market, yet he is still valued at the full sum the Torah fixes.

סד״א נדר בערכך כתיב כל שישנו בדמים ישנו בערכין וכל שאינו בדמים אינו בערכין

For you might have said: since the verse says “a vow by your valuation,” whoever has a market worth has a valuation, and whoever has no market worth has no valuation. The verse seems to tie the two vows together, so one could think that a person worth nothing at sale cannot be valued either.

קמ״ל נפשות כל דהו

The Mishnah therefore teaches that “persons” includes anyone at all. The Torah speaks of the valuation of “persons” with no condition attached, so the fixed sum applies whatever the person would fetch.
