# Humanology: product summary for critique

**For the reviewer:** Please critique this product: its logic, features, dashboards, user experience, scientific honesty and business sense. It's a phone app, currently a clickable prototype (Version 5) with sample data. Each section says what the app should do. Section 13 says what is simulated or missing, and section 14 lists weak spots we already suspect.

## 1. The idea

Humanology helps people try small, research-backed habits and **see what changes in their own health while they do them**.

The core loop:
1. Choose what you care about.
2. Get a few small habits that fit your time and comfort.
3. Do them and check in with one tap.
4. See how each habit lines up with the outcomes it is meant to help.
5. Keep what seems to help. If nothing changes after a few weeks, try the next habit.

The promise is "try it and see", never "this will work". Nothing is guaranteed, and the app must never present a pattern in someone's data as proof of cause.

## 2. Who it's for and how it should feel

- Everyday people who want better heart health, sleep, mood and so on, without needing wearables or medical numbers to start.
- Warm and calm. Few words, large painted illustrations, phone first.
- A recurring cast of seven painted characters (Nora, Theo, Ama, Walter, Priya, Marco, Sam).
- Cream, sage, butter and peach colors. Serif headings.
- No guilt: missed days never wipe progress, and there is no health score.

## 3. Content and evidence

- 135 habit entries with 277 research findings from two research reports. The content is provisional and not independently verified.
- Only 39 habits are currently cleared to be added to a plan. The rest can be read about.
- Eight goals: Heart health; Sleep & recovery; Mood & stress; Energy & focus; Strength & mobility; Nutrition & blood sugar; Digestive comfort; Memory & learning.
- Plain evidence labels: Well studied, Early research, For some people, Not recommended. Tapping a label explains it.
- Each benefit has its own grade (strong, moderate, preliminary, observational, not established). A habit can be strong for one outcome and weak for another.
- Three levels of detail: the how-to, "The science", and full research details.
- Each habit has a "Start small" version and an "As studied" version.
- A five-bar **Effort** rating (Very easy to Very challenging). It describes how demanding the habit is, not how well it works. The scores are editorial and need review.

## 4. Getting started

1. **Goals:** pick one or several, or "overall health".
2. **Anything in particular?** One short screen per chosen goal, skippable.
3. **How much time do you have?** Four stopwatch cards: Under 5, 5–10, 10–20, 20+ minutes, or No preference. This is the **total daily time for all habits**.
4. **What feels right?** Keep it gentle, A little movement, More effort, Stay seated, or Anything is fine.
5. **Recommendations:** usually three habits (up to five if many goals), one large card at a time with the next peeking behind it. Each shows benefits, time, evidence and effort, with "Add this habit" or "Maybe later". Nothing is added automatically. Going over the time budget asks the person to swap, add anyway, or keep their limit.
6. **Here's what you've chosen:** pick Every day, Weekdays or 3 days a week for each, remove any, then "Let's start".

## 5. Today (the main page)

In order:
- **A one-question check-in when relevant.** After a sleep habit, the next morning asks "How did you sleep last night?" (1–5). After a mood or energy habit, it asks how you feel. For heart habits, it prompts a blood pressure reading if there is none in four days.
- **Today's habits** as swipeable cards: Done, Not now (move, skip, rest day, pause, remove), How to, Follow along (guided timer or breathing pacer), With someone.
- **Find a habit** shortcut.
- **This week:** completed out of planned, with a status symbol for each day.
- **Your connections:** one plain sentence, for example "Sleep quality was a little higher on days you did earlier caffeine: 3.9 vs 3.0 out of 5."

After "Done", the person sees a mood check-in and an optional private reflection. When everything is finished, a large resting illustration replaces the cards.

## 6. Connecting habits to outcomes (the heart of the product)

| Habit's main goal | Outcomes tracked beside it |
|---|---|
| Sleep | Sleep quality, hours slept |
| Heart | Blood pressure (top number), resting heart rate |
| Mood | Mood, stress |
| Energy | Energy, sleep quality |
| Strength & mobility | Energy, steps |
| Nutrition | Energy, blood pressure |
| Digestion | Mood |
| Memory | Energy, mood |

Each habit shows up to two outcomes.

Data sources: one-tap check-ins, manually entered readings, and (in the future) Apple Health or Android Health Connect.

How comparisons work now:
- **Days you did it vs scheduled days you didn't.** Needs at least 4 done days and 3 not-done days with data. Rest days are excluded.
- **Before you started vs the last 3 weeks.** If there's no earlier data, the first 3 weeks are the starting point (after 6 weeks of use).
- Sleep is matched to the evening before it. A blood pressure or heart rate reading the next morning also counts for that day.
- Small differences read as "about the same".
- Only the person's own data is used.

Every view carries: "This shows what happened alongside the habit, not proof that it caused it."

## 7. Dashboards and what each should answer

| Screen | Question it answers | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Today | What do I do now, and is anything changing? | Check-in, habit cards, week progress, clearest connection |
| Habit page, "Your results with this habit" | Is this habit lining up with anything? | One card per outcome: sentence plus 8-week chart (bars = how often done, line = outcome) |
| Connection detail | What exactly changed? | Done vs not-done, before vs recent, chart, try-next advice, method, add a reading |
| Connections | Which habits seem to matter for me? | Every habit with its outcomes side by side |
| Progress | Am I doing what I planned? | Week circles or month calendar, completed/upcoming/skipped/missed/rest, "4 of 5 planned" per habit |
| One habit's history | How consistent have I been? | Week, month and all-time totals, calendar, schedule changes, reflections |
| Your health | What are my body measures doing? | Sleep, steps, resting heart rate; week and month history with gaps; states for not connected, declined, empty, loading, stale and error |
| Sleep and caffeine timeline | What happened night by night? | Each evening's check-in beside that night's sleep |
| You | My overview | Week summary, entries to Progress and Circles, mood, measures, rewards, look-back tools |
| Weekly review | What should I change next week? | Last week's results, then one decision: keep, make easier, reschedule or swap |

Past check-ins can be corrected for 7 days. Progress is judged against the schedule in force on each date. A repeated tap counts once.

## 8. Explore

- A scrolling list of large illustrated cards with goal filters and search.
- Each card: name, one benefit line, time, effort, evidence label, benefit tags.
- Tapping opens the habit; returning keeps the filter and scroll position.

## 9. My plan

- Picture-first list of habits with edit, pause, reorder and remove.
- "Stacks" group habits around one cue, such as after lunch.
- Rest days can be planned.

## 10. Doing it with other people

- Private, invitation-only circles for friends, partners and family.
- "Do this with someone" is prominent on the habit page and on Today.
- Shared check-ins, reactions (Cheer, Nice work, Keep going) and group milestones that never reset.
- Sharing is opt-in for each habit. Sleep, heart rate, weight, reflections and other health data are never shared.
- Leave a circle, stop sharing, revoke invite links, block, report, control notifications.
- People can create **personal habits**, always labeled "Personal habit · Not evidence-rated".

## 11. Rewards and money (proposed, undecided)

- Members earn up to 1,000 points per billing period for keeping one routine. 1,000 points = $1; a $5 gift card needs 5,000.
- No extra points for harder exercise, better moods or better health numbers.
- Example reward categories: Coffee, Books, Groceries (no real partners).
- The prototype shows $6.99 a month. A later idea is $7.99 monthly or $49.99 annually. These conflict and need a decision.
- Other ideas to test: one free habit vs a full-access trial, a guest pass for invited friends, a free month for referrals who pay, a pooled rewards budget of $1 per paying subscriber per month, and employer or insurer pilots without rewards (about $20 per member per year, exploratory).

## 12. Planned build and later ideas

- Native iPhone and Android app (React Native, Expo, TypeScript) with Supabase for accounts, schedules, check-ins and rewards; Apple Health and Health Connect; short character videos.
- Backlog only: sharing a health claim to get a sourced explanation, personal experiments, instant guided activities, recipes and shopping lists, character stories.

## 13. What the prototype does not really do

- No accounts, backend, notifications, payments, gift cards, invitation links or device connections. All are simulated.
- All health and history data is sample data. It was deliberately nudged so the connection screens have something to show.
- Screen readers and Safari are untested.
- Guided activity timings, habit wording, effort scores and research content still need professional review.
- A few illustrations are still missing.

## 14. Weak spots we already suspect (please challenge these and find others)

1. **Cherry-picking:** the Today card shows whichever habit has the largest difference. With little data, that is often noise.
2. **Thin evidence:** 4 done days and 3 not-done days is very little. The "about the same" cut-offs are judgment calls, not statistics.
3. **Confounding:** people may skip habits on bad days, run several habits at once, or improve for unrelated reasons.
4. **Direction:** results say "higher" or "lower" without saying whether that is welcome. A worse result could be featured.
5. **Poor outcome matches:** digestion is measured by mood, and memory by energy and mood. Nothing tracks digestive symptoms, memory, blood sugar, cholesterol, weight or balance.
6. **No real "experiment" structure:** there is no chosen outcome, baseline period, trial end date or clear keep, change or stop verdict. We think this may be the most important missing feature.
7. **Overlap:** Connections, habit results, an older Compare screen and the sleep timeline do similar jobs. Progress and Habit history overlap too.
8. **Confusing names:** "Connections" means both habit insights and (in Settings) device connections, and health connection appears in two places.
9. **Too much on Today?** A check-in, habit cards, progress and insights may crowd a page meant to be simple.
10. **Logging burden:** insights depend on frequent check-ins and readings that people may not enter.
11. **Rewards:** is $1 of value per month motivating, and do rewards encourage false check-ins?
12. **Safety:** what should happen if blood pressure or mood readings are worrying? The app currently shows no ranges or escalation guidance.
