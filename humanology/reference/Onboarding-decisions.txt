Approved multi-goal habit recommendations
When someone selects several goals, show multiple relevant habit recommendations rather than collapsing the result to one next-best habit. For heart, sleep and mood, generally present three distinct options. Cover the selected goals and clearly identify overlapping benefits without duplicating the same habit.
Preserve the spacious, one-habit-per-page experience: one large illustrated habit card in focus.
Introduce the set with “Three habits picked for you” and “1 of 3.” Adapt both labels to the actual number of recommendations.
Show relevant benefit badges, such as “Sleep · Mood,” on each card. Use only benefits supported by the habit’s evidence.
Support horizontal swiping, position dots, and visible previous/next controls. Preserve selections when navigating back and forth.
Offer “Add this habit” and “Maybe later.” Recommendations are choices, not automatic enrollment in all three habits.
After the set, show “Here’s what you’ve chosen,” a compact summary and a “Let’s start” button. Allow edits before confirmation.
Approved app-wide standard: peeking cards
For swipeable habit sets throughout Humanology, keep the next card visibly peeking out from behind the current card. This is the standard visual cue that more cards are available, not an optional onboarding treatment. Keep the current card dominant, with a clearly visible offset edge of the following card. Pair the peek with a position indicator and tap navigation; do not rely on gesture discovery alone. On the last card, show the end of the set rather than suggesting a nonexistent next card. For a single card, omit the peek and pagination. Keep text and controls readable at larger text sizes and respect reduced-motion preferences.
Recommendation time-budget detail to resolve
The proposed flow treats the time answer as the total daily routine budget, not a separate allowance for every recommended habit. Confirm that wording and calculation before implementation; avoid quietly turning a five-minute preference into three five-minute commitments. If all chosen goals cannot fit, explain the tradeoff and let the user choose rather than silently exceed the budget.
Approved Explore page: filtered, scrolling habit cards
Explore is a prominent destination for discovering and adding habits beyond the onboarding selection. Keep Explore in the bottom navigation. The approved primary browsing layout is a vertically scrolling list of illustrated cards, rather than one full-screen habit at a time.
Place goal filters across the top: All, Heart health, Sleep, Mood, and the remaining supported goals. Clearly indicate the selected filter. Selecting a goal displays all available habits relevant to that goal.
Use substantial, full-width illustrated cards stacked vertically in a continuous scrolling list. The number visible is flexible: begin with roughly two to three per typical phone viewport and choose the final proportions by visual review, prioritizing generous artwork and readable information over a fixed count. Avoid a grid of small tiles. Give each card a generous image area beside a clearly organized information area: habit name, one concise action or benefit sentence, time involved, and a plain-language evidence-level badge. Show relevant benefit tags when they fit neatly. Evidence labels must reflect the reviewed evidence for the stated benefit; tapping the badge opens its explanation. Keep deeper research, instructions and scheduling in the detail view. Do not shrink artwork or text merely to force three complete cards onto smaller screens or at enlarged text sizes; allow the next card to continue below the fold. There is no fixed three-card requirement or pagination. The proposed front-card fields are a design starting point: prioritize the action and benefit, then concise time and evidence cues; omit secondary tags if they crowd the layout.
Tapping a card opens the full illustrated habit detail view, with access to the evidence and the action to add the habit and choose its schedule.
Preserve search alongside goal-based browsing so people can find a specific habit directly.
Keep the selected filter and browsing position when returning from habit details.
Layout roles: Explore uses a vertical list for scanning and comparing. Onboarding recommendations and Today use large peeking cards for the smaller selected set. Preserve the circular habit illustrations in the weekly schedule for an overview. The peeking-card standard applies to horizontal swipeable sets; it does not require turning Explore into a full-page carousel.
Explore card effort indicator
Add a compact five-bar effort indicator to habit cards, alongside the time and evidence information. Fill one to five bars and leave the others visibly unfilled. Pair the bars with a short text label: Very easy, Easy, Moderate, Challenging, or Very challenging. Use the label “Effort” so this cannot be mistaken for a quality or evidence rating. Expose the label and numeric value to assistive technology; do not convey the level by color alone.
Rate the effort of performing the specific habit or variant, not its health benefit or evidence strength. Define a consistent editorial rubric before assigning scores; a short action is not automatically easy, and seated does not automatically mean low effort. Present the score as a general guide, since individual experience varies. Keep the indicator visually secondary to the illustration, action and benefit.

Habit progress and health tracking
Add a tracking experience to V5 with a proposed entry called Progress within the You tab. Link each active habit to its own history. Use the existing warm palette, readable live text and circular character thumbnails throughout.
Keep the weekly schedule with circular habit illustrations. Tapping a day shows its scheduled habits and their status. Distinguish completed, upcoming, skipped and unscheduled days with symbols and labels as well as color.
Make a completion easy to record from Today, with undo and correction of past check-ins. Let people review a week or month and tap a habit to see its completion calendar and totals.
Show progress against scheduled opportunities, such as 4 of 5 planned sessions completed. Use the schedule that applied on each date so changing a schedule preserves historical meaning.
Offer a brief optional reflection about how the person feels. Give reflections their own visible label and keep the check-in quick.
Your health placeholder

Reserve space for Apple Health on iPhone and Health Connect on Android in the future native app. The V5 HTML should demonstrate this flow with clearly labeled sample data and a connection preview.
Each connected-state card shows its metric, unit, measurement date, source and a compact weekly trend. Tapping opens a larger history with week and month controls.
Design unconnected, permission declined, connected with no readings, loading, stale data and connection error states. Missing readings appear as gaps. Offer retry and connection settings where relevant.
Include a small related-metric card within relevant habit details. For an evening caffeine-cutoff habit, show a sleep card such as Last night, 7 h 20 min, plus a small weekly trend. Mark example values as sample data in the prototype.
Tapping the related card opens a shared timeline showing sleep measurements alongside caffeine-cutoff check-ins. Label the sleep date consistently and associate the evening action with the following sleep episode. Show observations in neutral language and avoid attributing a change to a habit from the timeline alone.
Provide measurement details and connection controls. People explicitly choose data access. Health measurements and reflections are private by default, with any future sharing requiring an explicit choice.
