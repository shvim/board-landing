# Homepage heroes: interior-design software and best-in-class SaaS/creative tools (captured 8 Nov 2026)

Method note for the report writer: every "live homepage" finding below comes from a headless-Chromium capture made on 8 Nov 2026. Each site was captured at 1440x900 and at 390x844 (deviceScaleFactor 1, iPhone user agent). Hero text was pulled from the DOM, and only the text that fell inside the first viewport counts as "hero"; the next 900px counts as "first scroll". Screenshots are in `research_notes/Loupe landing page research/screens/` and are named `<site>-desktop.png` / `<site>-mobile.png`. Copy in quotation marks is verbatim, including the sites' own capitalisation and typos. For motion I sampled frames at 2.5s, 5s, 8s and 11s and measured the share of pixels that changed between frames. Several sites run A/B tests or change their copy by viewport, and I flag those where I saw them.

Sites I could not capture: Studio Designer and Mydoma Studio served a Cloudflare "Confirm you are human" page to the headless browser. I read Studio Designer's text from its raw HTML with curl, so I have its copy but no screenshot. Mydoma rate-limited me (HTTP 429), and the old mydoma.com domain now redirects to a domain-for-sale page. Canva's interior-design page and lovable.dev also returned bot checks. The WebFetch tool could not resolve any of these hosts, so the trade-press and teardown pages were read through the same headless browser.

## Q1a. Interior-design software heroes: verbatim copy, CTAs, visual, motion, proof, free vs paid

### Takeaway
Interior-design tools lead with business-management promises (time, admin, growth, FF&E, procurement) and use studio and firm language. Their CTAs are mostly demo- or trial-led. Only the consumer and "visual" players (Mattoboard, Spoak, Canva, Morpholio Board, Material Bank) say "free" loudly, and none of the heroes is about saving and finding inspiration. The heroes that look best (Programa, Spoak, Mattoboard, Gather) put physical materials or real product UI in the first viewport rather than stock interiors.

### Inferences
- Headline length ranges from 3 words (Programa, Morpholio) to 9 (DesignFiles, Studio Designer). The best-looking heroes (Programa, Spoak, Mattoboard, Material Bank) pair a short line with tactile, real-world material imagery: swatches, samples and boxes. Business-suite incumbents (DesignFiles, Studio Designer, Houzz) use long, outcome-stuffed headlines and stock or room imagery.
- Free vs paid splits by buyer. Platforms sold to firms push demos (Gather: demo only; Houzz: demo first, trial second; Studio Designer: demo plus "Purchase"). Mid-market tools push trials (Programa's 7-day trial, DesignFiles' free trial). Tools for individuals or creative work push "free" (Mattoboard, Spoak, Material Bank, Canva, Morpholio's free download). Loupe's "free to use" is unusual for a tool aimed at professionals and could be a point of difference if it is stated plainly in the hero.
- Social proof in this category is press logos (Mattoboard, Spoak, Morpholio), aggregate numbers (DesignFiles, Houzz, Mattoboard's "300k designers") and named studio principals (Programa, Studio Designer). Logo walls of design firms are rare; Gather's "TRUSTED BY LEADING DESIGN FIRMS" is the exception.

### Gaps
- No screenshot or visual description for Studio Designer or Mydoma (bot protection). Studio Designer's copy came from raw HTML.
- I could not reach a Canva page specific to interior design (bot check). Only the generic mood-board page was captured.
- Houzz Pro's free-trial length and pricing were not confirmed on the page. A search summary mentioned a 30-day trial on paid tiers, but I did not verify it.
- I found no reliably reported 2025–2026 launch of an AI moodboard tool for interior designers. Search results were aggregator listings (Moodboardly, Oda Moodboard, MoodBoard AI) of uncertain quality ([search result example](https://toolradar.com/tools/moodboardly/alternatives)). Mattoboard (DesignStream AI) and Spoak are the most visible current consumer-facing entrants I could verify.

## Q1b. Best-in-class SaaS and creative-tool heroes: verbatim copy, CTAs, visual, motion, proof, free vs paid

### Takeaway
The admired heroes in late 2026 have short headlines (4–7 words is typical), a single primary CTA (usually "Download…" or "Get … free"), real product UI or a product demo as the hero visual, and little or no social proof above the fold. Logo walls usually arrive in the first scroll. Motion is used to show the product working (agents typing, notes being "enhanced", a rotating verb) rather than as decoration. Nearly every product now presents itself through AI or agents.

## Q4. What interior designers say about these tools, especially about inspiration and reference management

### Takeaway
Designers praise tools that save time, look professional to clients, and let them reuse a product library. They complain about clunky mood-board tools, crashes when pulling web images, no cross-device sync, curated catalogs they will never use ("we have our own vendors"), and messy shared access. Inspiration habits are improvised: Pinterest, Dropbox plus InDesign, phone photos from trade showrooms, and private boards. Several designers distrust client Pinterest dumps and want the designer to be the curator.
