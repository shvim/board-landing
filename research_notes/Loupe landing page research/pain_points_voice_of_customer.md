**How to read these notes (method and limits, please read first)**

Reddit was also blocked: scripted requests were refused, and WebSearch refuses `reddit.com` as a domain filter. **Every quote below therefore comes from search-engine result snippets, not from the full page.** Those snippets are machine-summarized, so text shown in quotation marks is *probably* verbatim but must be checked against the linked page before it goes on the landing page. To verify the Business of Home and Futurism quotes, businessofhome.com and futurism.com have to be allowed in the session's network settings.
- Quote labels:

  - **ID** interior designer

  - **SO** studio or firm owner, or a firm's account
  - **C** consumer or non-designer

- Strength of evidence:

## Q0. Which themes recur most and most strongly, and when do designers feel them? (ranked synthesis)

### Takeaway
The loudest pain from 2024 to 2026 is that **the place designers kept their inspiration (Pinterest) got worse and turned out not to be theirs**. Feeds filled with AI-generated rooms and ads, and in 2025 accounts with years of boards were wrongly banned. Underneath that is the older, everyday pain of **not being able to find a saved image at the moment of need**: images are scattered across Pinterest, Instagram saves, screenshots, bookmarks and folders, none of which can be searched the way a designer describes an image. The studio-level pain is logical, and vendor case studies hint at it, but it is under-evidenced.

### Cited Findings

**Ranked themes.** Rank is my judgment from frequency and emotional intensity in the sources found.

| Rank | Theme | Frequency in sources | Strength of language | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pinterest degraded: AI-generated interiors, ads, shopping pivot, spam | Very high, with sustained press 2025 to 2026 | High ("depressing", "unusable", petitions, quitting) | FT (Jul 2025), Wired (late 2025), Futurism (Feb 2025), YPulse (Nov 2025), Engadget, Tom's Guide, Creative Bloq; Pinterest added AI controls specifically for home decor and architecture |
| 2 | Your library isn't yours: bans, deletions, dead links | High in spring and late 2025 | Very high ("library of Alexandria has been burned down", "10 Years of Professional Work Lost") | Fast Company, TechCrunch, Pinterest Community threads incl. a design firm's account |
| 3 | Can't find what I saved: scattered storage, no search | Medium-high (blogs, guides, vendors) | Medium | gatherit, Instagram-collections guides, screenshot-tool blogs |
| 4 | Lost provenance: can't trace an image back to the product or source | Medium | Medium | Tailwind, G2 Pinterest reviews, refern (2026) |
| 5 | Client inspiration is noisy: unbuildable AI rooms, homogeneous Pinterest picks | Medium | Medium ("dreads") | Business of Home (two pieces), AOL/HGTV, Mumsnet |
| 6 | Studio and team knowledge: per-person or per-office image silos, offboarding, house style | Low (mostly vendor case studies, job postings) | Low to medium | OpenAsset case studies (ZGF, Foster + Partners), junior job postings |

- Pinterest admitted in May 2025 that accounts were "mistakenly deactivated" because of an "internal error" [S, J/V]. — [TechCrunch, 14 May 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/14/pinterest-finally-admits-mass-bans-were-a-mistake-caused-by-an-internal-error/)
- The FT (Bethan Staton, c. July 2025) reported that Pinterest "is increasingly filled with AI interior design images, often featuring unrealistic or impossible details, complicating users' search for inspiration" [S, summary of the FT by TheFly]. — [TipRanks/TheFly summary of FT](https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/pinterest-filled-with-ai-interior-design-images-ft-reports-thefly)

**Triggering moments: when do they feel it?** This is the evidence-backed list.
1. **Building a client moodboard or presentation.** The designer goes to Pinterest or stock and has to wade through AI images. A garden designer (OD) said AI images make client inspiration boards hard to use, describing "plants that literally don't exist" [S]. — see Q1 and Q5
2. **Sourcing the actual product in an image.** The pin links to Google Images, a spam page or a 404, so the product can't be bought. — [Social Media Today via search](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/content/why-its-no-longer-safe-click-pinterest-images); [G2 Pinterest reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/pinterest/reviews_and_filters)
3. **The client sends their own Pinterest board.** It is full of AI rooms or of images chosen for the wrong reason. — [Business of Home, "How do you really feel about Pinterest?"](https://businessofhome.com/articles/how-do-you-really-feel-about-pinterest)
4. **Opening the app and finding the account suspended.** This happened in the April and May 2025 wave and continued into Nov and Dec 2025. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91329035/pinterest-users-fuming-over-sudden-bans); [Pinterest Community](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/my-pinterest-account-was-suspended-by-mistake-10-years-of-professional-work-lost/40372)
5. **"I know I saved it."** The designer scrolls hundreds of screenshots or an Instagram collection that has no search. — see Q3
6. **A staff member leaves, or a studio grows past one office or one person's taste.** This rests on vendor case studies only. — see Q4

### Inferences
- For a landing page, the **sharpest, most current emotional hook** is theme 1 plus theme 2 together: "Pinterest isn't a library and was never yours." The **most universal, evergreen hook** is theme 3: "you saved it, and now you can't find it when the client is waiting." Theme 1 explains why people are searching for an alternative now (the trigger). Theme 3 is the job they hire the product to do every day.
- Themes 2 and 6 are the bridge to the buyer, the studio owner. A wrongly banned *design firm* account ("10 Years of Professional Work Lost") is the best real-world proof that a studio's visual knowledge can sit in one person's personal platform account and disappear.

### Gaps
- I could not count Reddit threads (blocked), so the frequency ranking leans on press and blog coverage, which over-represents the Pinterest story because it is newsworthy. Primary interviews or a scrape of r/InteriorDesign and r/interiordesigners from a network that allows Reddit would validate the ranking.

## Q1. What do designers say about losing or not finding inspiration images they saved? (client meeting, moodboard, sourcing, "I know I saw it somewhere")

### Takeaway
The evidence describes the everyday pain clearly: images are spread across many places, Instagram collections can't be searched, screenshots are named by timestamp, and pins lose their source. However, I found few first-person interior-designer quotes about the "I know I saw it" moment, and the best ones are probably on Reddit, which I couldn't reach.

### Cited Findings
- Designers juggle "browser bookmarks, handwritten notes, clipped magazine pages, and several Pinterest boards," so the source recommends one central hub [S, J/V; interior-design organizing blog]. — [gatherit.co](https://gatherit.co/?p=607)
- An interior designer said they had repeatedly lost track of pins because they never downloaded them to a named file, and the images "vanished" into their computer. Their workaround is to download favourite pins to the desktop and file them in a named folder [P, ID]. The snippet's attribution to a specific page in the result set was unclear; the likely source is the UK interior design studio blog. — [fifimcgee.co.uk](https://fifimcgee.co.uk/blog/interior-design-mood-board) (verify)
- In the screenshot-library world (UX designers), the common pain is described as "you scroll through 400 PNGs named Screenshot 2026-02-14 at 3.41.12 PM.png. You never find it." [S, J/V; vendor blog, OD]. — [PagePouch blog](https://pagepouch-web.vercel.app/blog/how-to-build-ux-inspiration-library-you-can-search)
- Pins are not an archive. "When a linked page disappears, the Pin thumbnail remains while the original source context is gone" [S, J/V; 2026 vendor review of moodboard tools for interior designers]. — [refern.app](https://www.refern.app/blog/interior-designer-moodboard-tool)
- Sourcing failure: pins often come from Google Images, which links back to Google rather than to the maker, "leaving product shoppers with no way to buy" [P]. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/content/why-its-no-longer-safe-click-pinterest-images). Reviewers report that many pins redirect to broken or low-quality sites, including 404s and unrelated spam [P, C]. — [G2 Pinterest reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/pinterest/reviews_and_filters). The standard workaround is reverse image search in Google Images or TinEye [P]. — [Tailwind](https://www.tailwindapp.com/blog/how-to-find-the-original-source-of-a-pinterest-pin/amp?p=269)
- Moodboard moment, client side: designers (ID) in a Business of Home roundup said they prefer "oblique images" to the "hero images that endlessly circulate on Instagram and Pinterest." One said the algorithm encourages clients "to compile a diluted, homogenous crop of images" [S, ID, Feb 2023]. — [Business of Home, "7 designers on how they make the most of inspiration images" (3 Feb 2023)](https://businessofhome.com/articles/6-designers-on-how-they-make-the-most-of-inspiration-images)
- One designer in that roundup always asks clients what specifically draws them to an image instead of only collecting pictures [P, ID]. — [Business of Home (2023)](https://businessofhome.com/articles/6-designers-on-how-they-make-the-most-of-inspiration-images)
- Sourcing time, which is where finding an image ends up: one vendor estimates designers running 4 to 6 projects spend "ten to fifteen hours per project on sourcing tasks" [S, J/V]. — [Monaverse](https://monaverse.com/blog/ai-furniture-sourcing-interior-design). Another vendor puts sourcing at 15 to 30% of project hours, with no method given [P, J/V]. — [Studio Designer](https://www.studiodesigner.com/blog/how-can-interior-designers-source-quality-products-faster/)

### Inferences
- The "I know I saw it somewhere" moment is strongly implied by the scattered-storage and no-search evidence, but **I could not find it stated in a quotable first-person interior-designer voice**. Treat it as a hypothesis to validate in customer interviews; the founder's own users are the best source.
- "Find it by describing it" maps directly onto two documented failures: Instagram has no keyword search inside collections, and screenshots are named by timestamp. It also maps onto the sourcing moment, if Loupe keeps the source URL with each image.

### Gaps
- No verbatim Reddit or Houzz-forum quotes from interior designers about losing saved images. Both were blocked; Houzz did not surface in search.
- No App Store review text for mymind, Eagle, Milanote or Cosmos was retrievable; only third-party summaries.

## Q2. Pinterest frustrations 2024 to 2026: AI images, ads and shopping, algorithm, lost pins and sources, low-quality reposts

### Takeaway
This is heavily documented. Press coverage specifically names **home decor and interior design** as among the categories most flooded with AI imagery. Pinterest's own response (AI labels, an "AI tuner", and per-category controls that include home decor and architecture) confirms the problem is real at scale. Pinterest is also openly repositioning itself as an AI shopping assistant.

### Inferences
- The Pinterest story gives Loupe a **"why now"**: designers' default tool has become (a) polluted with fake rooms, (b) a shopping and ads surface, and (c) a landlord that can evict you. Copy that names those three without naming Pinterest ("no ads, no AI-generated rooms, and it's yours") will feel current.
- Loupe is a private library, not a feed, so AI slop is *less* of a problem inside Loupe. The real-world pain is that designers **save** AI images by mistake and **lose the source** of real ones. Keeping the source URL and making provenance visible could be a credible differentiator. That is an inference, not something designers asked for in the sources.
- Be careful: designers themselves are split on AI (see Q6). Copy that attacks AI generally could clash with Loupe's own "AI tags every image" feature. Frame it as "AI that organizes *your real* references," not "anti-AI."

## Q3. Screenshots, camera roll, folders, bookmarks, Instagram saves, WhatsApp, Slack and email as storage

### Takeaway
Instagram "Saved" works as an informal moodboard, but it is fragile and hard to search. Screenshots are the default capture method and become an unsearchable heap. Desktop folders are the "safe" workaround designers recommend to each other. I found no designer-specific evidence about WhatsApp, Slack or email as image storage.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram's Saved folder is described as "the modern moodboard" [V title, J/V]. — [Byline, "The Instagram Saved Folder is the Modern Moodboard"](https://www.bylinebyline.com/articles/saved-folder-1)
- Instagram collections get so large that "it becomes sometimes difficult to search them," with no sort or search inside a collection [S, OD; UX case study]. — [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/ux-design-case-study-creating-a-more-organized-experience-on-instagram-ca8516a4ac49)
- "Users cannot directly search for posts within a specific collection based on keywords or hashtags" [S, J/V]. Other guides say a Saved-page search bar exists, so sources conflict on this. — [Dewey guide](https://getdewey.co/blog/instagram-saved-posts-guide/)
- "If the original creator deletes a post, it will disappear from your saved collection as well," so screenshots are suggested as backup [S, J/V]. — [Dewey guide](https://getdewey.co/blog/instagram-saved-posts-guide/)
- Suggested workaround: build granular collections grouped by visual element such as color, texture or composition [P]. — [Dewey guide](https://getdewey.co/blog/instagram-saved-posts-guide/)
- Screenshots: "you scroll through 400 PNGs named Screenshot 2026-02-14 at 3.41.12 PM.png. You never find it." [S, vendor, UX context]. — [PagePouch](https://pagepouch-web.vercel.app/blog/how-to-build-ux-inspiration-library-you-can-search). A whole category of screenshot-organizer apps exists (Shoots, Screenshotter, Nicelydone with text search inside screenshots), which shows demand. — [Shoots, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1616477228); [Nicelydone](https://nicelydone.club/)
- Designers use the camera roll itself as a visual diary. Designer Liz Caan (ID) draws inspiration from her own phone photos rather than other people's feeds [P; about 2020, older]. — [Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/how-to-find-design-inspiration-36889305)
- Folder and tag systems are the standard advice for interior designers: tag each saved file so it can be found and shared later [P]. — [Boston Design Guide](https://www.bostondesignguide.com/?p=43678); folder structures plus a DAM like Eagle [P] — [Eagle blog](https://eagle.cool/blog/post/how-to-organize-interior-design-inspirations-and-files)
- The "download to a named folder" workaround after losing pins (ID, attribution to verify). — [fifimcgee.co.uk](https://fifimcgee.co.uk/blog/interior-design-mood-board)
- Physical analogues persist. Erin Napier (HGTV) uses magazine collages [P]. — [House Digest](https://housedigest.com/1676609/hgtv-erin-napier-magazine-home-inspiration). Firms shelve design books by visual category such as style or era [P, vendor]. — [Coohom](https://www.coohom.com/article/how-interior-design-firms-build-and-organize-their-design-book-libraries)

### Inferences
- The mobile capture moment (Instagram, the camera roll on a site visit or in a showroom) is where images enter the chaos. Loupe's iOS share sheet answers this directly, so the landing page could show "Share → Loupe" from Instagram or Photos.
- "Instagram deletes it, Pinterest bans you, screenshots bury it" is a defensible three-part summary of what goes wrong with each current storage method, each backed by a source above.

### Gaps
- No sources found on interior designers using WhatsApp, Slack, email or iMessage threads as reference storage. The behaviour is plausible, especially with clients, but undocumented here.
- No quantification of how many screenshots or saves designers accumulate.

## Q4. Studio and firm-level problems: references on individuals' phones and accounts, knowledge lost when staff leave, team sharing, onboarding juniors to taste, client presentations

### Takeaway
There is **thin direct evidence from interior design principals**. The best signals are:
1. A design firm's 10-year Pinterest account wrongly suspended, so the firm's references lived on a third-party personal-style account.
2. Architecture-firm DAM case studies describing per-office image silos replaced by a firm-wide reference library.
3. Junior job postings showing studios treat libraries and house "design language" as something juniors must maintain and absorb.

The buyer-side pain is plausible but needs primary validation.

### Cited Findings
- A design firm's account: "My Pinterest Account Was Suspended by Mistake – 10 Years of Professional Work Lost" (Nov 2025) [V title, SO]. — [Pinterest Community](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/my-pinterest-account-was-suspended-by-mistake-10-years-of-professional-work-lost/40372)
- ZGF (architecture) previously had a dedicated image server in each studio and moved to a shared repository used mainly to find "reference material and precedents" [P, vendor case study]. — [OpenAsset / ZGF case study](https://fr.slideshare.net/Axomic/axo078-openasset-casestudyzgfv100eke)

- Institutional know-how is the real loss when people leave. A figure attributed to Gartner says about 42% of institutional knowledge sits solely with individuals [S; secondhand, unverified]. — [Dan Cumberland Labs](https://dancumberlandlabs.com/blog/best-master-architecture/); see also [Medium/Arazoo, "How much is lost each time someone leaves your firm?"](https://medium.com/arazoo-team/how-much-is-lost-each-time-someone-leaves-your-firm-f41e3c9942a5)

Postings list "upkeep of a fabric library", helping "with the ID library", "management of design library", and developing concepts "that align with VDV design language" [S, SO via job postings]. — [Business of Home job board](https://businessofhome.com/jobs/junior-interior-designer-aaa89970-8dcd-4558-82d4-c135b4b4c56a); [Business of Home job board](https://businessofhome.com/jobs/junior-designer-a9f46de9-ec34-44f1-964b-61c9561da2d5); [Mediabistro](https://www.mediabistro.com/jobs/1833676917-junior-designer)

- Designers should eventually become the client's main source of inspiration, beyond Pinterest boards [P, ID panel]. — [Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/how-do-you-really-feel-about-pinterest)

### Inferences
- The buyer story that can be defended from evidence: **"Your studio's taste currently lives in personal Pinterest accounts, phones and one person's screenshots. One ban, resignation or lost phone and it's gone."** The firm-account ban is the real proof point. The "staff leaving" angle is logical, and architecture firms solved it with DAMs (ZGF, Foster + Partners), but those tools are enterprise-priced and built for project photography, not inspiration. That leaves a gap for small interior studios. The last point is an inference.
- "Onboard a junior to the studio's eye in a day" is an attractive buyer message. The evidence shows studios *care* about house design language, but not that they currently struggle to transfer it. Validate before leading with it.

### Gaps
- No verbatim quotes from interior design principals about inspiration living on employees' accounts, staff departures taking references with them, or version chaos in client presentations. Business of Home's business coverage, ASID/IIDA forums and principal interviews would be the next sources; Business of Home was blocked in this session.
- No interior-design-specific data on staff turnover cost related to reference libraries.

## Q5. How designers describe images, and whether generic search and tagging fails them

### Takeaway
Designers sort and talk about images by **style, era, material, colour, texture and composition**, and by kind of shot ("oblique" vs "hero"). AI-tagging tools (mymind) promise search by object, colour and style, but a review notes the tags are **too broad for specialists**. I found no direct designer complaint that Pinterest, Google Photos or Apple Photos search fails on terms like "travertine" or "fluted". That is a gap, not a negative finding.

### Cited Findings
- Designers distinguish "oblique images" from "hero images that endlessly circulate" [S, ID]. — [Business of Home (2023)](https://businessofhome.com/articles/6-designers-on-how-they-make-the-most-of-inspiration-images)
- Firms shelve reference books by visual category such as style or era [P, vendor]. — [Coohom](https://www.coohom.com/article/how-interior-design-firms-build-and-organize-their-design-book-libraries)
- Advice to group saved references by colour, texture or composition [P]. — [Dewey guide](https://getdewey.co/blog/instagram-saved-posts-guide/)
- Studios describe their aesthetic in compact vocabulary, e.g. "timeless, balanced, and layered" (Studio Todd Raymond), and require work in the studio's "design language" [S, SO via job postings]. — [Business of Home job board](https://businessofhome.com/jobs/junior-interior-designer-8041e4f7-ea3c-4fd1-9bfb-59b08c30c272)
- The vocabulary designers use to spot fakes is material- and detail-literate: "asymmetrical Persian rug patterns", "AI glow" softening corners [S]. — [Check Out This House](https://checkoutthishouse.substack.com/p/beware-of-the-ai-pinterest-home)
- mymind (closest AI-tagging competitor) claims you can "search any object, color, detail, theme, style or text appearing within an image" [S, vendor]. — [mymind](https://mymind.com/?p=373). A user says they "don't tag or use folders and can find things easily by color, type, object" [S, likely testimonial]. — [mymind](https://mymind.com/?p=368)
- Limitation: a review says mymind's tags "can be too broad for specialists working with highly specific research material," and that it fails to recognise highly abstract art styles accurately [S/P, reviewer]. — [Saner.ai mymind review](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/mymind-reviews)
- Eagle (desktop DAM popular with designers) organizes by tags, ratings, colours and annotations; this is manual or semi-manual [P, vendor]. — [Eagle blog](https://eagle.cool/blog/post/collect-inspiration-tool)

### Inferences
- The "too broad for specialists" critique of mymind is the opening for Loupe's "designer language" tagging: "boucle", "travertine", "burl", "1970s Italian", "limewash", "Calacatta Viola" rather than "chair, brown, room". That inference is supported directly only by the mymind review, so the landing page should *show* example tags rather than claim superiority.
- Pairing colour names with materials and eras is distinctive vocabulary (e.g. "oxblood lacquer, 1970s, moody"). A search-box demo with a designer-style query is likely the fastest way to convey this.

### Gaps
- No sources found where interior designers explicitly say Pinterest, Google Photos or Apple Photos search fails on material or finish terms. This needs testing or interviews (e.g. ask designers to find a saved "fluted travertine side table" in their current system).

## Q6. Data on time and money lost searching for files, information and assets (general, creative, interior design)

### Takeaway
General knowledge-work studies consistently put information-searching at **roughly 2 to 10 hours a week (about 20 to 25% of time)**, depending on definition. Creative and marketing DAM surveys, mostly vendor-funded, cluster around **3 or more hours a week** locating assets. I found **no interior-design-specific study** measuring time lost finding inspiration images. Houzz industry reports cover demand and costs, not workflow.

### Cited Findings
**General knowledge work**
- McKinsey Global Institute (2012): employees spend about 1.8 hours a day, roughly 9.3 hours a week, searching and gathering information [S, via secondary]. — [Playbook blog](https://www.playbook.com/blog/time-wasted-searching-for-files/)
- APQC: 2.8 hours a week looking for or requesting information, rising to 8.2 hours including recreating or duplicating existing work. Workers with enterprise search spend about 0.7 hours a week [S]. — [APQC](https://www.apqc.org/blog/km-makes-knowledge-workers-more-productive-and-less-stressed-out)
- IDC: about 2.3 to 2.5 hours a day, about 25% of work time [P, secondary; figures vary by citing source]. — [KMWorld](https://www.kmworld.com/Articles/Editorial/Features/What-are-people-searching-for-and-where-are-they-looking-41012.aspx)
- Atlassian State of Teams 2025 (12,000 knowledge workers, 200 Fortune 1000 executives): leaders and teams "waste 25% of their time searching for answers". There is an estimated 2.4 billion hours a year wasted in the Fortune 500, and 43% say they could work faster if information were easier to find [S]. Some outlets report 27%. — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/state-of-teams-2025); [UNLEASH](https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/atlassian-ai-could-help-workers-stop-wasting-2-4-billion-hours-searching-for-information-every-year/); [Deviniti (secondary for 43%)](https://deviniti.com/blog/leadership-teamwork/35-system-of-work-statistics/)

**Creative and marketing asset search (mostly vendor research)**
- Canto and Ascend2: 57% of content and marketing professionals (US/UK) estimate they lose more than three hours a week locating assets [S]. — via [Playbook](https://www.playbook.com/blog/time-wasted-searching-for-files/) and DAM search results
- Brandfolder: 62.5 hours a year searching for brand assets (2015 data, older) [S]. — [Brandfolder](https://brandfolder.com/blog/time-and-money-its-all-about-your-dam-roi)
- Forrester, cited by AdManage: creative teams waste 8.3 hours a week searching for assets [S; not verified against Forrester]. — [AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/creative-planning-asset-management)
- Frontify: marketing teams lose 35 minutes a week searching for and sharing assets [S, vendor]. — [Frontify](https://www.frontify.com/guide/unlocking-content-efficiency)
- Smartsheet State of DAM 2023: 76% of DAM users report less time spent searching for digital assets [S, vendor]. — [Smartsheet](https://offers.smartsheet.com/2023-state-of-digital-asset-management-2023.html)
- A Demand Metric/BrandCentral (2020) figure of "90+ hours" is an obvious outlier or misreport; do not use.

**Interior-design-specific**
- Sourcing: 10 to 15 hours per project (Monaverse, vendor) and 15 to 30% of project hours (Studio Designer, vendor); neither gives a method [S/P]. — [Monaverse](https://monaverse.com/blog/ai-furniture-sourcing-interior-design); [Studio Designer](https://www.studiodesigner.com/blog/how-can-interior-designers-source-quality-products-faster/)
- Houzz 2025 U.S. State of the Industry (1,537 renovation businesses): revenue and profit fell in 2024; 58% of interior designers expect stronger demand in 2025 [S]. — [US Lighting Trends](https://www.uslightingtrends.com/news/contractors-architects-and-designers-share-insights-in-the-2025-houzz-state-of-the-industry-report/); [Houzz PDF](https://st.hzcdn.com/static/econ/2025_US_Houzz_State_of_the_Industry.pdf)
- Houzz UK: 85% of UK interior designers reported higher cost of doing business in 2024 [S]. The top 2025 challenges for UK interior designers were finding prospective customers (50%) and the economy (39%) [S]. — [Houzz UK 2025 PDF](https://st.hzcdn.com/static/econ/2025_UK_Houzz_SOTI.pdf); [Houzz UK 2026 PDF](https://st.hzcdn.com/static/econ/UK2026HouzzSOTI.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a landing-page stat, the most defensible recent figure is **Atlassian 2025: about a quarter of the workweek spent searching for information** (large sample, recent), or APQC's narrow 2.8 hours a week. Avoid the 8.3-hour Forrester and 90-hour figures.
- A designer-specific claim like "designers lose X hours a week looking for images" would need Loupe's own survey. Rough illustrative maths for a studio: at 2.8 hours a week (APQC) for 5 designers at a $75/hr billable rate, that is about $1,050 a week of non-billable time. This is a hypothetical calculation, not a sourced fact, and must be labelled as such if used.
- Houzz data says designers' top business worries are **winning clients and rising costs**. For the buyer (studio owner), frame Loupe around **better, faster client presentations** (winning and keeping clients) and **protecting studio IP**, not only "save time".

### Gaps
- No ASID, IIDA, Business of Home or Houzz survey found on time spent organizing or finding inspiration images, or on tools used for moodboards.
- No Adobe or Canva survey specific to inspiration management surfaced in searches.

## Q7. Jobs to be done: what designers hire such a tool for, what triggers a search, what makes them switch, and what anxieties stop them

### Takeaway
- **Job:** "When I'm building a scheme or presentation, or sourcing, let me instantly pull up the right real reference I saved, from anywhere, so I look prepared and decisive in front of the client. And make sure it's still there next year and belongs to my studio."
- **Triggers:** Pinterest getting worse (AI slop, ads), a ban or lost library, a scramble before a client meeting, and team growth.
- **Switching pull:** ad-free, no AI slop, one-click Pinterest import, and search without filing.
- **Anxieties:** ownership and lock-in (ironically heightened by Pinterest), privacy of client and project images, AI accuracy for specialist vocabulary, cost, and "another app".

### Cited Findings
**Push away from the current tool (triggers)**
- Pinterest's AI and ads problems and the 2025 bans (see Q2). Users are reported moving to Cosmos and Tumblr [S, secondary]. — [PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2025/11/24/pinterest-ads-spam/)
- Designers wading through AI images on stock sites "adding more time to their workflows" [S, OD]. — [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/ai/ai-art/designers-say-ai-is-making-stock-image-sites-unusable)

**Pull toward alternatives (what the switchers praise)**
- Cosmos is described as an "ad-free, anti-AI slop platform that posits itself as the taste maker" [S]. — [Creative Bloq, best Pinterest alternatives 2026](https://www.creativebloq.com/creative-inspiration/the-best-pinterest-alternatives-in-2026). TechRadar: "there are no ads or sponsored content" [S]. — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/computing/websites-apps/this-app-is-like-pinterest-if-it-were-cooler-and-ad-free)
- Importing Pinterest boards is part of Cosmos's appeal for designers [P]. — [Wix Studio](https://www.wix.com/studio/blog/cosmos-app)
- Are.na and Savee are listed as ad-free, curated alternatives [P]. — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/creative-inspiration/the-best-pinterest-alternatives-in-2026)
- mymind's pull is no folders and no tagging; you find things by colour, type and object [S, testimonial]. — [mymind](https://mymind.com/?p=368)
- Milanote positions on "compositions" vs Pinterest "collections", i.e. notes and to-dos beside images [P, vendor]. — [Fabric comparison](https://fabric.so/comparison/pinterest-vs-milanote)

**Anxieties and objections**
- *Ownership and lock-in:* platform bans wiped "years of carefully curated boards" [S]. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91329035/pinterest-users-fuming-over-sudden-bans). Instagram saves vanish when the creator deletes the post [S]. — [Dewey](https://getdewey.co/blog/instagram-saved-posts-guide/). The implied counter-anxiety is "will a new app also hold my library hostage?" (inference). Download-as-zip and export address it.
- *Privacy:* screenshot apps market "100% on your phone and doesn't connect to our servers" as a feature [S, vendor]. — [Screenshotter, via search](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1616477228). Designers at a Las Vegas Market panel are embracing AI but "remain cautious about authenticity, data privacy and creative control" [S]. — [Home Accents Today (design trends)](https://www.homeaccentstoday.com/category/design-trends) (attribution to verify)
- *AI accuracy for specialists:* tags "too broad for specialists" [S]. — [Saner.ai review](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/mymind-reviews)
- *Cost and free-tier limits:* mymind's upgraded tier is listed at $12.99/month or $129/year [S]. — [mymind pricing](https://www.mymind.com/pricing). Milanote's free plan is capped at 100 notes, images and links in total [S]. — [Capterra / GetApp listings](https://www.getapp.com/collaboration-software/a/milanote/reviews)
- *Designers' ambivalence about AI itself:* designers are split. Many see AI as an efficient way to get ideas, while others find AI imagery frustrating [P]. — [AOL/HGTV](https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/hgtv-meet-chatgpt-ai-influencing-100011570.html)

### Inferences
- **Suggested JTBD statements.** These are inferences built from the evidence above.
  - Individual designer: "When a client asks 'what did you have in mind?', help me pull up the exact references I saved, by describing them, so I look prepared and the scheme moves forward."
  - Individual designer: "When I see something on Instagram, in a showroom or on a site visit, let me save it in one tap, with its source, so it's findable later."
  - Studio owner: "Make sure the studio's accumulated taste is a company asset (shared, searchable, exportable) and not trapped in personal accounts and phones."
  - Studio owner: "Let a new hire see how we think visually in their first week."
- **Switch triggers to target in copy and ads:** "Pinterest is all ads and AI rooms now"; "I lost my boards"; "I couldn't find that image in the client meeting"; "we just hired our second designer".
- **Objection-handling content that the evidence suggests is needed:**
  - "Your library is yours: export everything as zip anytime." This answers lock-in, which the Pinterest bans made salient.
  - "Private by default; client images stay in your studio."
  - "Tags in designer language, and you can edit them." This answers AI-accuracy doubts.
  - "Works where you already save (Chrome, iOS share sheet) and drops into Canva and Figma." This answers "another app".
- **Messaging risk:** "AI tags every image" plus anti-AI-slop messaging could read as contradictory. Lead with the outcome ("find it by describing it") and frame AI as quiet plumbing.

### Gaps
- No first-person switching stories from interior designers (e.g. "I moved from Pinterest to X because...") were retrievable. Reddit and App Store reviews would supply these.
- No data on willingness to pay among interior designers or studios for inspiration tools.
